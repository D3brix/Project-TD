# Tower System & Elemental Progression

## Core Tower Philosophy

The game should eventually contain a large number of towers and tower
possibilities, but that complexity should be revealed gradually.

A tower is not defined only by its element. The system can separate:

-   **Tower archetype** --- how the attack is delivered.
-   **Element/attribute** --- what the attack does.
-   **Upgrades** --- how that tower specializes.
-   **Elemental reactions** --- how attacks interact with other
    elements.
-   **Progression** --- when the player gains access to those systems.

This allows a relatively small number of underlying systems to produce a
very large number of strategies.

> **Individual towers are strong. Tower combinations are stronger.**

### Upgrade paths describe how a tower attacks (prototype, Phase 2.5)

A tower's upgrades are a choice of direction, not a fixed sequence that makes every
fully upgraded tower the same. From its base level the player commits the tower to one
path; the other paths are then closed for that tower (sell it and build another to
change direction). The current prototype paths are:

-   **Rapid** --- many light attacks. Scales attack speed; hits stay weak.
-   **Heavy** --- few powerful attacks. Scales hit damage; stays slow and overkills.
-   **Balanced** --- moderate gains in damage, speed and range, with no extreme.

These describe *how* the attack is delivered, not an element, so a future element can
combine differently with a Rapid tower than with a Heavy one.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Tower Archetypes + Elements

Tower archetypes determine attack behavior.

Examples:

-   Ballista
-   Cannon
-   Mage
-   Totem
-   Rapid-fire tower
-   Area-control tower
-   Support tower

Elements can then modify what those archetypes do.

### Example: Ballista

**Physical Ballista** - High single-target damage. - Piercing potential.

**Fire Ballista** - Long-range bolts ignite enemies.

**Water Ballista** - Applies Soaked and may knock enemies backward.

**Lightning Ballista** - Heavy electrical strike that can chain under
the right conditions.

This creates many possible towers without requiring every tower to be
designed as an unrelated unit.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Elements Are Progression Unlocks

Elemental towers should **not all be available from the beginning**.

The player begins with a simpler version of the combat system and
gradually discovers additional elements through progression systems such
as a skill tree, research tree, world progression, challenges, bosses,
or other milestones.

Early gameplay can focus on physical mechanics:

-   Damage
-   Range
-   Attack speed
-   Piercing
-   Armor
-   Explosives
-   Area damage
-   Targeting
-   Positioning

Only after the player understands these foundations does the elemental
system begin to open.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Progression Must Have Tempo

Major systems should not unlock constantly.

If Fire, Water, Frost, Lightning, elemental reactions, prestige, Light,
and Darkness all appear quickly, the game burns through one of its
strongest sources of long-term progression.

Each major unlock needs time to breathe.

The player should:

> **Introduce → Learn → Experiment → Master → Pressure → Reward**

### Example

1.  **Introduce Fire**
2.  Player learns burning and Fire damage.
3.  Player experiments with Fire towers and upgrades.
4.  Player becomes comfortable using Fire.
5.  New enemies/world conditions begin challenging Fire-heavy
    strategies.
6.  The player gains access to Water.
7.  Water creates new solutions.
8.  Eventually Fire + Water interactions become available.

The next mechanic arrives when the player is beginning to want another
tool --- not immediately after the previous unlock.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Progression Eras

These are conceptual pacing stages, not final hour requirements.

### Era I --- Foundations

The player learns traditional tower-defense fundamentals.

Available concepts may include:

-   Physical towers
-   Ballistics
-   Piercing
-   Explosives
-   Armor
-   Basic tower upgrades
-   Positioning
-   Target priorities

The game is intentionally understandable at this stage.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Era II --- First Element

The player discovers their first true element, such as **Fire**.

Fire introduces:

-   Burning
-   Damage over time
-   Elemental resistance
-   Anti-regeneration possibilities
-   Fire-specific upgrades

The player spends meaningful time learning Fire before another major
element appears.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Era III --- Elemental Expansion

A second element such as **Water** becomes available.

Water may introduce:

-   Soaked
-   Knockback
-   Pressure
-   Cooling
-   Conductivity interactions later

Initially, Fire and Water can still function mostly independently.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Era IV --- Elemental Interaction

The player discovers that elements can interact.

This should feel like a major revelation rather than something available
immediately.

For example:

**Water → Fire**

Creates:

### Steam / Fog

-   Creates a fog cloud.
-   Enemies inside may become Confused.
-   Example effect: -20% movement speed.

Potentially, order can matter.

**Fire → Water**

Could instead create:

### Thermal Shock

-   Rapid cooling causes burst damage or another specialized effect.

This creates multiple strategies from the same two elements.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Elemental States

Elements can apply temporary states rather than requiring two attacks to
land on the exact same frame.

Example:

**Water hit**

> SOAKED --- 3 seconds

If Fire hits while Soaked is active:

> STEAM

This makes elemental combinations deliberate and understandable.

Possible states include:

-   Burning
-   Soaked
-   Frozen
-   Electrified
-   Corroded
-   Rooted
-   Cursed
-   Marked

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Example Elemental Reactions

  Combination         Reaction      Possible Effect
  ------------------- ------------- ----------------------------------------
  Fire + Water        Steam         Fog, confusion, movement reduction
  Water + Lightning   Conduct       Increased chaining / electrical damage
  Water + Frost       Freeze        Temporary immobilization
  Fire + Nature       Wildfire      Spreading damage over time
  Nature + Water      Overgrowth    Roots / vines / battlefield control
  Nature + Poison     Toxic Bloom   Poison spreads to nearby enemies
  Fire + Earth        Magma         Persistent burning terrain
  Frost + Earth       Brittle       Armor becomes easier to break
  Lightning + Metal   Magnetize     Pulls or clusters enemies
  Fire + Lightning    Plasma        Burst damage / armor penetration

These are design directions rather than finalized reactions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Three-Element Reactions

Later progression can introduce more advanced combinations.

Example:

**Water → Frost → Physical Impact**

### Shatter

A frozen enemy struck by a powerful physical attack:

-   Takes major burst damage.
-   May damage nearby enemies.
-   May lose armor.

Three-element reactions should be substantially later than basic
two-element reactions so the combat system continues gaining depth over
time.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Elements Should Have Identities

Elements should not simply be differently colored damage types.

### Fire

-   Burning
-   Spreading
-   Heat
-   Combustion
-   Anti-regeneration

### Water

-   Soaking
-   Pressure
-   Knockback
-   Cooling
-   Conductivity setup

### Frost

-   Slowing
-   Freezing
-   Brittleness
-   Control

### Lightning

-   Chaining
-   Burst
-   Conductivity
-   Overload

### Earth

-   Armor breaking
-   Terrain interaction
-   Stunning
-   Physical control

### Nature

-   Roots
-   Growth
-   Spreading effects
-   Support

### Poison

-   Damage over time
-   Infection
-   Weakening
-   Anti-healing

### Wind

-   Knockback
-   Projectile manipulation
-   Spreading other elemental effects

Additional elements can be introduced later as the game expands.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Light and Darkness --- Late-Game Attributes

**Light and Darkness should unlock much later than the normal
elements.**

They should feel like the player has reached another layer of the game's
combat system.

They should not simply function as yellow and purple damage.

### Light

Possible identity:

-   Purification
-   Marking
-   Amplification
-   Reflection
-   Energy manipulation

### Darkness

Possible identity:

-   Corruption
-   Sacrifice
-   Curses
-   Life drain
-   Entropy

Their existence may initially be hidden from the player.

Example late-game requirement:

> **??? --- LOCKED**
>
> Reach the required world.\
> Discover enough elemental reactions.\
> Complete mastery challenges.\
> Defeat a major boss or event.

Only then could the Light/Darkness branches reveal themselves.

### Light + Darkness

A possible advanced reaction:

> **ECLIPSE**

The exact mechanics can be designed later, but interactions between
Light and Darkness should feel unusually powerful or mechanically
strange.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Skill Tree Philosophy

The skill tree should unlock **mechanics**, not only statistics.

Simple statistical nodes still have a place:

-   +5% Fire damage
-   +10% Burn duration
-   +1 Lightning chain target

But major nodes should change gameplay.

Example progression:

**Pressurized Water**

> Water attacks can apply Soaked.

↓

**Conductivity**

> Lightning gains additional effects against Soaked enemies.

↓

**Elemental Reactions**

> Compatible elemental states can now react.

↓

**Dense Fog**

> Steam slows enemies more strongly.

↓

**Lingering Mist**

> Fog remains on the battlefield after affected enemies die.

The player should feel as though they are unlocking new rules, not
merely increasing numbers.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. The Skill Tree Can Grow

The entire progression tree does not need to be visible at the
beginning.

Early game may show only:

``` text
PHYSICAL
├── Ballistics
├── Piercing
└── Explosives
```

Later:

``` text
PHYSICAL ───────── FIRE
    │                │
    │                ├── Burning
    │                └── Combustion
    │
    └──────── ELEMENTAL THEORY
```

Much later, previously hidden branches can reveal themselves:

``` text
                  [ ??? ]
                     │
        ┌────────────┴────────────┐
      LIGHT                    DARKNESS
```

The physical expansion of the skill tree can itself become a progression
reward.

The desired feeling is:

> **"Wait... there's more?"**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 12. Environment + Element Interactions

World conditions should participate in the same elemental system.

Example:

### Heavy Rain

All enemies periodically become **Soaked**.

Effects could include:

-   Fire buildup reduced.
-   Lightning conductivity greatly increased.
-   Frost buildup increased.

This is more interesting than simply giving Fire towers a flat damage
penalty.

The environment can therefore create or modify elemental states and
reactions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. Pacing Principle

Unlocking a new element should create more than one progression moment.

For example:

> Unlock Fire\
> → Master Fire\
> → Unlock Water\
> → Master Water\
> → Discover Fire + Water reactions\
> → Upgrade those reactions\
> → Encounter enemies that change how those reactions work

Two elements can therefore support many hours of meaningful progression.

The game should avoid:

> Unlock → Unlock → Unlock → Unlock

Instead:

> **Introduce → Learn → Experiment → Master → Pressure → Reward**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14. Long-Term Combat Evolution

The player's understanding of combat should evolve alongside
progression.

### Early Game

> Tower → Enemy → Damage

### Mid Game

> Tower → Element → Status → Resistance → Reaction

### Late Game

> Tower → Element → Status → Reaction → Environment → Chain Reaction →
> Elemental Mastery → Light/Dark Mechanics

A late-game player should be able to look back at the first few levels
and realize they were playing a deliberately simplified version of a
much larger combat system.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Core Design Principles

> **Progression does not only make the player stronger. It progressively
> reveals the depth of the game's rules.**

> **Individual towers are strong. Tower combinations are stronger.**

> **New elements should expand the usefulness of existing elements, not
> simply replace them.**

> **Major mechanics need enough time to be learned and mastered before
> the next major layer is introduced.**

The goal is for the game to begin as an approachable tower defense game
and gradually unfold into a deep elemental and incremental strategy
system.
